# B10 workflow

The B10 scientific path runs one ordinary producer Job per C0 repeat and up to three sequential producer Jobs per C1 repeat. Each of the five repeats produces nonfinal evidence. A final phase sweep then classifies the timeout cohorts and restart arms, and the judge publishes a candidate containing the decision-rule selection.

The [approved scientific three-Job plan, Revision 7](https://github.com/collaborationwithothers/eventhubs-kafka-dotnet/blob/4196a0325fd91534239efbc70ef3dbafd6dfe10e/items/404/plan-b10-scientific-three-job.md), sections 3.1 to 3.8, owns this cutover. The [abrupt contract](b10-abrupt-contract.md#scientific-execution) owns scientific names, purposes, chart admission and C1 verifier evidence; the [verifier contract](verifier-contract.md#scientific-c0-verify) owns C0 verification. The [approved final judgement plan, Revision 6](https://github.com/collaborationwithothers/eventhubs-kafka-dotnet/blob/07e2f2a3d80d9d0342590919d7a349f38a0d546a/items/404/plan-b10-final-judgement.md), sections 3.3 to 3.6, owns finalization. The legacy two-Job path and diagnostic trial remain documented separately below.

## Scientific execution

Scientific prepare generates run IDs matching `^b10-[0-9]+-[0-9]+$`, called S, using `b10-{GITHUB_RUN_ID}-{GITHUB_RUN_ATTEMPT}` within the existing run-ID length limit. For each repeat N from 1 to 5, C0 runs `start-line`, `loadgen`, `verify`, with one producer and no `loadgen2`. C1 runs the following sequence for run ID `R`:

| Order | Job suffix after `R-c1-rN-` | Admission and failure behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `start-line` | Runs unless the phase has stopped. An outcome other than `complete` records `b10_abrupt_publication_failed` at `metadata` and ends the repeat, subject to the existing `run_mode` safety stop after an unknown/apply failure that cannot establish no remaining pod, or deadline exhaustion after an applied Job that did not complete. Metadata is then checked as below. |
| 2 | `loadgen` | Requires accepted metadata. A render failure records `b10_abrupt_configuration_invalid` at `timeout_job` and ends the repeat without setting `safety_stop`. `unknown`, `apply_failed` or a failed termination pod read records `b10_abrupt_timeout_job_unknown`, sets `safety_stop` and stops the phase. `complete` or `failed` with the pod check passed continues. |
| 3 | `loadgen-restart` | Requires the timeout step to continue. `unknown` or `apply_failed` sets `safety_stop` regardless of the pod read. The first-termination pod read always runs; a render failure, apply failure or failed read gives `b10_abrupt_first_termination_unknown` and sets `safety_stop`. A first-publication identity mismatch has the same safety result. Other noncomplete outcomes after a passed pod read record `b10_abrupt_publication_failed` at `first_termination` and end the repeat. A rejected first publication records its failure at `first_publication` and ends the repeat with no successor or verify. |
| 4 | `loadgen-successor` | Requires accepted first publication, including `successor_permitted=true` and a nonempty kill set. A late release records `b10_abrupt_successor_late`. Render failure or a noncomplete outcome records `b10_abrupt_publication_ambiguous` at `successor_job`, or `b10_abrupt_deadline_exhausted` for wait exit 124. `unknown` or `apply_failed` also sets `safety_stop`. Every applied successor receives the trial's publication and receipt checks during `run-repeats`; a failed pod read gives `b10_abrupt_first_termination_unknown` and `safety_stop`, while other failures retain their trial names at `successor_publication` or `successor_receipt`. |
| 5 | `b10-abrupt-verify` | Requires accepted first publication and runs after the successor step whatever that step recorded, including `safety_stop`. A noncomplete outcome records `b10_abrupt_verify_failed` at `verify_job`, preserving `safety_stop`. Download of the [scientific C1 result](b10-abrupt-contract.md#scientific-per-repeat-c1-verifier) follows; a failed download records `b10_abrupt_verify_failed` at `verify_collection`. |

A phase stop takes precedence over a repeat end. Once `safety_stop=true`, no later repeat's Job is applied and every later slot is recorded with reason `safety_stop`; only the current repeat's verifier may still run under its admission rule. A repeat end skips the remaining Jobs of that repeat and permits the next repeat to start. A no-kill end, empty kill set or rejected first publication starts neither successor nor verifier.

Before each C1 timeout Job, the phase downloads `R/C1/rN/start-line.json`, `broker-inventory.json` and `start-line-completion.json`. Trial metadata checks apply with repeat N, receipt Job `R-c1-rN-start-line`, start-line identity Job `R-c1-rN-loadgen` and the scientific C1 expected-name list owned by the abrupt contract. A missing object records `b10_abrupt_publication_ambiguous`; other metadata rejection records `b10_abrupt_publication_failed`, at `metadata`, ending that repeat. Success creates configmap `R-b10-abrupt-first-rN`; the successor uses `R-b10-abrupt-successor-rN`. Their binding formats remain the trial's.

At most one producer pod runs at a time across repeats. Each C1 producer's termination is confirmed before the next producer is rendered, or the phase stops. After every applied Job, `collect-job` copies its folder using the [trial collection mapping](b10-abrupt-contract.md#failure-names-and-collection); collection failures record their existing names and do not themselves end the repeat or phase. All repeats share the unchanged 18000-second orchestration deadline and 360-minute workflow job timeout. Deadline exhaustion records later slots as `orchestration_deadline`.

After each applied producer or verify Job in either configuration, the phase appends one object to `phase-status.json` under `b10_lifecycle.producers` or `b10_lifecycle.verifiers`. Each object has exactly `{job, repeat, mode, pod_name, pod_uid, finished_utc, exit_code}`, taken from that Job's one pod observation; `finished_utc` is normalized from `state.terminated.finishedAt` by `b10_utc`. Missing termination evidence instead appends a gap `{object: "<job>/pods.json", reason: "producer_termination_unavailable"}` or reason `verifier_termination_unavailable`. A Job not applied adds nothing. Lifecycle pod reads share a cumulative 120-second bound for the phase. Any lifecycle gap makes `run-repeats` exit 1.

`run-repeats` also exits 1 for an outcome in `BAD_OUTCOMES` or any recorded failure name; otherwise it exits 0. It sets no diagnostic trial marker in scientific `phase.json`, so the existing diagnostic-state rejection remains in force. The legacy `loadgen2`, legacy sweep preparation and `b10-sweep` code remain unreachable for B10 science. After `run-repeats`, the existing `judge` command runs `b10-sweep-line`, `b10-final-sweep` and `judge`, in that order, for both configurations. Every phase Job has no repeat number and uses the same absolute orchestration deadline as the repeats.

Before the capture Job, scientific sweep preparation reads the retained `b10_lifecycle`. It requires exactly one producer entry for every producer Job recorded as applied and exactly one verifier entry for every applied per-repeat `verify` or `b10-abrupt-verify` Job, with no gaps or extra entries. A successor whose render failed contributes no applied Job or lifecycle entry; its applied verifier still requires one. No applied producer, a missing entry or a gap gives `lifecycle_evidence_gap` and skips `b10-sweep-line`.

The capture receives the latest producer `finished_utc` as `B10LastLoadgenTerminatedUtc` and the latest verifier `finished_utc` as `B10LastVerifierCompletedUtc`. If no per-repeat verifier was applied, the latter is the latest producer time. The phase waits until the later of that verifier time and 60000 ms after the latest producer termination. This is the existing harness settle policy, not an Event Hubs latency guarantee.

`R-<phase>-b10-final-sweep` is a verifier-role Job on `workload` with the existing 750-second verifier Job deadline, 9300000-record and 429496729600-byte phase budgets. These are configured limits from the [approved plan, section 3.3](https://github.com/collaborationwithothers/eventhubs-kafka-dotnet/blob/07e2f2a3d80d9d0342590919d7a349f38a0d546a/items/404/plan-b10-final-judgement.md), not measurements. The chart requires an S run ID, scenario B10, phase C0 or C1 and no repeat. Trial run IDs are rejected. The [final sweep projection](b10-abrupt-contract.md#final-sweep-projection) owns the final classifications, independent restart eligibility, closed output and completion receipt.

The Blob-only judge authenticates that projection and its receipt and writes `R/P/candidate.json`. The [scientific candidate contract](verifier-contract.md#scientific-b10-final-judgement) owns the decision rules and candidate fields. `candidate` downloads and checks the candidate through the existing bounded retrieval path; failed retrieval removes stale candidate and partial files and leaves `full_allowed=false`. A candidate's `rule_selection` is an input to Hari's separate verdict approval, not publication authority. The temporary final-judgement gap literals remain defined but are unused for S.

This change authorizes no dispatch or scientific publication. The approved plan's section 7 requires a separately authorized governed diagnostic run of the scientific path after this change and the final-judgement change merge, before any scientific run. Synthetic local checks do not establish live behavior.

## Legacy execution outside S

The remaining legacy workflow sections describe the retained schema-one two-Job path for run IDs outside S. They do not describe the scientific sequence above.

### Legacy inputs and execution

The [scenario](../../scenarios/B10/scenario.yaml) selects phases C0 and C1, with five repeats each. Both use `message.timeout.ms=100` and `max.in.flight=5`; C0 inherits `enable.idempotence=false`, and C1 overrides it to true. The [synthetic timeout profile](../../scenarios/profiles/b10-timeout.yaml) retains the saturation profile's seed, tenant mix, payload distributions and stage rates, with four 10-second stages. Its configured event and attempt bounds, rather than observed live counts, are checked in [the implementation receipt](../../items/355/receipt.md).

Each repeat runs start-line, the first producer, the second producer and verify, in that order. The first producer permits its existing single application retry for a permitted timeout. The second producer discovers the complete predecessor publication before registration or Kafka construction and resends the selected failed attempts once, without application retry. The workflow observes actual first-pod termination before launching the second Job; a successful process exit alone cannot authenticate a publication. The existing worker owns its 30-second predecessor discovery budget.

The metadata-only `b10-sweep-line` Job follows all repeat slots. The workflow takes the latest actual terminated `finishedAt` from the ten retained producer pod observations and the latest completion time from the five bound verify receipts. Each observation is attempted once. Retrieval and parsing share a 120-second budget, with a 65536-byte limit per receipt. Missing, malformed or unbound lifecycle evidence leaves an explicit gap and no invented sweep note.

The workflow waits until the later of the last verifier completion and the last producer termination plus 60000 ms. This is an explicit harness settle budget, not a bound on Event Hubs append latency. Capture receives `B10LastVerifierCompletedUtc`, `B10LastLoadgenTerminatedUtc` and `B10SettleIntervalMilliseconds`; it does not wait or orchestrate. It captures document-events partition highs, cleans up its Kafka session, and creates the note once. The existing [sweep-note contract](verifier-contract.md#b10-phase-sweep-finalization) owns timing, schema, identity and byte validation. The host's final completion receipt follows host disposal; uncertain create-only publication is not retried or overwritten.

The `b10-sweep` Job then authenticates and reconciles the repeats. Judge remains the final separate step. B10 verify's nonfinal failure exit remains a diagnostic failure; the workflow does not relabel it as successful scientific evidence. An unknown live pod prevents subsequent launches until existing deletion and confirmation establish safety.

All repeat modes, lifecycle observations, settling, capture, sweep and judge share one absolute 18000-second orchestration deadline. Operations use only its remaining time. Exhaustion records unattempted modes and gaps, stops new producers, preserves available diagnostics and leaves cleanup to the workflow. This cap is shorter than the conservative configured worst-case envelope; it does not guarantee successful completion. Missing or failed sweep evidence cannot authorize finality or counts.

### Legacy diagnostic events

Sweep pod logs contain structured events with `scenario_id`, `run_id`, `phase`, `source_repeat` and `observation_complete`:

| Event | Additional fields |
| --- | --- |
| `B10LateObservationSummary` | `observation_available`, `total_selected_late_records`, `retained_samples`, `omitted_samples` |
| `B10LateObservationGroup` | `instance_id`, `partition`, `landing_repeat`, `selected_late_records` |
| `B10LateObservationLocation` | `instance_id`, `attempt_id`, `partition`, `offset`, `landing_repeat` |

A complete observation requires the whole phase scan to succeed and that source repeat to reconcile successfully. Unavailable totals remain absent/null rather than fabricated zeros. Partial observations are explicitly marked incomplete and cannot support a complete frequency claim. Diagnostic projection starts after the scan timer is disposed, Kafka cleanup finishes and finalization is published. Provider exceptions cannot change the published finalization bytes or their outcome counts.

Projection still runs before the handler returns and the host publishes its completion receipt. It shares the worker's inherited 720-second process lifetime with evidence reads, scanning, cleanup and publication. A slow projection can exhaust that lifetime: the watchdog exits with code 124, the completion receipt can be absent, and the judge then rejects the sweep despite its published finalization. Moving projection after scanning removes its direct charge against the scan timer, not this process-lifetime coupling. See the [deployed limits](a01-runtime-limits.json), [watchdog](../../src/Parchwell.Verifier/VerifierWorker.cs), [host receipt publication](../../src/Parchwell.Verifier/Program.cs) and [judge acceptance](../../src/Parchwell.Verifier/Judge/JudgeHandler.cs). No runtime-viability guarantee follows from the synthetic local measurements in the [implementation receipt](../../items/355/receipt.md).

Only reconciled own late records are selected, with each topic/partition/offset counted once. The location sample retains the first 100 records per source repeat in partition/offset order. `omitted_samples` records the excess while complete grouped frequencies still cover every selected record. With two instances, 32 partitions and six landing labels, the group bound is 384 per source repeat. These are configured diagnostic limits from the [approved plan](https://github.com/collaborationwithothers/eventhubs-kafka-dotnet/issues/355#issuecomment-6669538298), not measured frequencies. Existing phase record and byte limits still control input work.

`landing_repeat` is a later repeat number only when the offset lies inside that repeat's recorded half-open original window; otherwise it is `gap`. An offset at a window's start is inside, and one at its end is outside. Logs carry producer instance and attempt identifiers, not tenant/document payloads or private Azure identities. A late record is not automatically a duplicate. Authenticated classifier totals and diagnostic frequencies answer different questions; logs cannot authorize the former.

### Legacy retrieval and later smoke record

Public Actions artifacts retain the `b10-C0` and `b10-C1` pod-artifact prefixes, including per-Job logs and `pods.json`. `phase-status.json` records attempted and unattempted workers and gap reasons, with B10 lifecycle observations under `b10_lifecycle`. Private workflow inputs are separate from public evidence.

During the run, the verifier container holds these fixed phase-root objects, where `U` is the run ID and `P` retains the original phase case:

- `U/P/sweep-line.json`: captured partition highs and supplied lifecycle timestamps.
- `U/P/b10-sweep-line-completion.json`: capture completion receipt, bound to `<U>-<lowercase-P>-b10-sweep-line`, cleanup success and the note's actual length/hash.
- `U/P/b10-finalization.json` and `U/P/b10-sweep-completion.json`: existing authenticated sweep output and receipt.
- `U/P/rN/verify-completion.json`: immutable repeat completion evidence.
- `U/P/candidate.json`: the judge's candidate; the workflow downloads the phase candidates under their B10 prefixes.

These objects remain in ephemeral run storage only for the run. Public pod logs are diagnostic artifacts, not replacement ledger evidence or authoritative outcome totals. Retrieval and retention follow the [verifier contract](verifier-contract.md) and [ADR-020](../blueprint.md#adr-020-the-workflow-owns-the-infrastructure-lifecycle-no-net-code-orchestrates).

A later authorized legacy smoke would retain each phase/repeat's permitted-timeout count and persistence status, first-instance retry count, second-instance resend count, late-record frequency and sampled locations including gaps, omitted sample count, guard failures, scan completion, worker gap/exit evidence and candidate authentication. Missing observations stay unavailable. A clean local harness test does not establish live behavior.

The scientific rule remains [ADR-021](../blueprint.md#adr-021-b10-accepts-timed-out-sends-application-retries-and-one-producer-restart-in-a-second-job-and-classifies-late-appends-up-to-a-sweep-line). Every later B10 publication must state: **A late append after the phase sweep line is not detected.** Expected outcomes remain successor work. Mutation acceptance is not met; a later passing main measuring run covering the changed target remains required before evidence publication.

## Diagnostic trial (inactive for science)

Diagnostic-only; not active for scientific publication. Hari dispatches `.github/workflows/b10-abrupt-trial.yml` on main with `confirm_diagnostic_trial` set to true and approves the `parchwell-scenarios` environment. It runs the phase commands `prepare-b10-abrupt-trial`, `run-b10-abrupt-trial` and `collect-b10-abrupt-trial`, for B10, C1 and repeat 1 only. After the start line, it runs the ordinary C1 timeout Job `{run}-c1-r1-loadgen`, then `{run}-c1-r1-loadgen-restart` with its parent and self-killing producer child, then `{run}-c1-r1-loadgen-successor` only after observed restart-pod termination and acceptance of the first publication with `successor_permitted=true`. After the successor step, it runs `{run}-c1-r1-b10-abrupt-verify` whenever the first publication was accepted, whatever the successor outcome. The [diagnostic verifier contract](b10-abrupt-contract.md#diagnostic-verifier-mode) owns that mode's inputs, nonfinal result and failure semantics. The next paragraph's ordering applies between producer Jobs; verification is a consumer and may run while successor termination is unknown. The successor's release is the authenticated `kill_intent` UTC plus 120 s. The cross-host timing conclusion is `not_verified` unless an independently checked clock-uncertainty bound exists, and none exists in this repository. Cleanup, ephemeral destruction and the dispatch-end AKS stop always run. The policy cap is one trial under [decision 6769760685](https://github.com/collaborationwithothers/eventhubs-kafka-dotnet/issues/404#issuecomment-6769760685); a second trial spends money and goes to Hari. [Decision 6787982173](https://github.com/collaborationwithothers/eventhubs-kafka-dotnet/issues/404#issuecomment-6787982173) leaves to Hari whether a trial stopped before the restart Job counts toward that cap. The trial cannot test fatal-capture completeness, the verifier's separation of an intended kill from a crash, broker persistence, pod or node loss, or a verdict.

At most one producer pod runs at a time. Each Job is waited for before the next is applied, and its pod is observed terminated before the next Job is rendered. Finished Jobs and their terminated pods are not deleted until cleanup.

The [Kubernetes Job page, Terminal Job conditions](https://kubernetes.io/docs/concepts/workloads/controllers/job/#terminal-job-conditions), read 2026-11-11, states that from v1.31 terminal conditions are added only after all Job pods terminate; [the cluster version validation](../../infra/cluster/variables.tf) requires at least 1.35.0. The trial verifies pod termination directly. After timeout wait outcome `complete` or `failed`, one pod read must show exactly one terminated timeout pod with its expected Job owner, `loadgen` container/status and image. Either outcome then permits the restart Job and remains recorded. A failed check, read exception, `unknown` or `apply_failed` stops later Jobs with `safety_stop=true` and `b10_abrupt_timeout_job_unknown` at `timeout_job`; `render_failed` stops them with `b10_abrupt_configuration_invalid` and leaves `safety_stop` unchanged. An exception elsewhere in that step uses fallback `b10_abrupt_publication_failed`. Unknown restart-pod termination also sets `safety_stop` and blocks the successor.

Collection follows the release path. Each applied timeout, restart, successor and diagnostic verifier Job has its `collect-job` logs copied to `artifacts/b10-abrupt-diagnostic/<job>/`; the start-line record is not copied. For a complete diagnostic verifier Job, collection also retrieves `b10-abrupt-diagnostic/nonfinal-result.json` under the [collection contract](b10-abrupt-contract.md#failure-names-and-collection). The timeout Job receives no ordinary verify or successor-receipt check. The workflow uploads only `b10-abrupt-diagnostic`. [ADR-021](../blueprint.md#adr-021-b10-accepts-timed-out-sends-application-retries-and-one-producer-restart-in-a-second-job-and-classifies-late-appends-up-to-a-sweep-line) owns the protocol, the [approved part 1 plan](https://github.com/collaborationwithothers/eventhubs-kafka-dotnet/blob/fc1a4f36452b2d302f1d73987e1ba5eb967b3d65/items/404/plan-b10-execution-loadgen.md) sections 3.1, 3.3 and 3.8 to 3.11 own the processes, names and publication, and the [approved part 2 plan](https://github.com/collaborationwithothers/eventhubs-kafka-dotnet/blob/0c73e5139e1670d5b675a51fc52dbd99c4b1af49/items/404/plan-b10-execution-trial.md) sections 3.1, 3.2 and 3.5 own this ordering, timeout handling and collection. The [B10 abrupt diagnostic contract](b10-abrupt-contract.md) records the schema-3 journal interfaces.

For trial run IDs matching `^b10d-[0-9]+-[0-9]+$`, the start line carries `{run}-c1-r1-loadgen`, `{run}-c1-r1-loadgen-restart` and `{run}-c1-r1-loadgen-successor`, in that order. Trial metadata rejects any other list before applying a producer Job. Scientific run IDs use the [scientific expected-name rules](b10-abrupt-contract.md#scientific-execution). The [abrupt diagnostic contract](b10-abrupt-contract.md#diagnostic-verifier-mode) owns these interfaces and the nonfinal classification output.

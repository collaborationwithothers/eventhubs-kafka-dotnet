# C01: a consumer killed before its first commit was replaced 31.8 to 38.6 s after the kill at session timeout 30000 ms and 61.3 to 68.2 s at 45000 ms; in every repeat some messages were marked done without being processed

In run 45124695288 on Event Hubs Premium, a consumer in a new two-member group was killed at its first message, before processing it, and the other member took over its partitions 31.8 to 38.6 s after the kill with `session.timeout.ms` 30000 and 61.3 to 68.2 s with 45000, in five repeats each. In every repeat, messages were marked done without ever being processed: 66 to 78 per repeat at 30000 and 125 to 141 at 45000. The proposed verdict is `contradicted` because takeover at 45000 exceeded the 15 s rebalance allowance, a policy value, in all five repeats; this does not show that either value is right or wrong, and the workload is synthetic.

Proposed verdict: `contradicted`.

## In plain words

**The production event.** A consumer in a brand-new group is killed before it has ever committed an offset, and the other member of the group takes over its partitions. Both consumers run this repository's baseline settings: `auto.offset.reset` `latest`, offsets committed automatically every 5000 ms, and each offset stored only after its message is processed. Messages keep arriving throughout. "Marked done" means the group's committed offset for a partition has moved past a message, so no member will read it again. The first setting (C0) uses `session.timeout.ms` 30000, Microsoft's recommendation for Event Hubs; the second (C1) gives only the killed consumer 45000, the librdkafka default, and the member that takes over keeps 30000.

**What happens, step by step.** The run measured the takeover time and the counts below; the steps that connect them come from the librdkafka documentation and source listed under Sources, and are an inference, not a measurement. The killed member sends no more heartbeats, so its partitions go unread until the broker's session timeout removes it from the group. Messages keep arriving on those partitions. The member that takes over finds no committed offset for them, because the killed member never committed one, so `auto.offset.reset` `latest` starts it at the end of each partition. Everything that arrived between the crash and the takeover is skipped. Once the survivor commits newer offsets, the group treats the skipped messages as done. No error is raised: with `latest` librdkafka resets on its own, and only the setting `error` raises an error; the consumers' logs are empty, so this was not observed in the run. The killed member committed nothing; the survivor's commits are what pass the skipped messages.

**What was measured.** With session timeout 30000 ms the other member held the killed member's partitions 31.8 to 38.6 s after the kill, and 66 to 78 messages per repeat were marked done without being processed. With 45000 ms on the killed member, the takeover came 61.3 to 68.2 s after the kill, and 125 to 141 messages per repeat were skipped. After each repeat the verifier read the group's committed offsets from Event Hubs and found them at the run's end line on every partition that received messages. Each repeat sent 600 synthetic messages over 150 s, and all five repeats of each setting were valid.

**What the verdict means and does not mean.** The prediction for each setting allowed 15 s of rebalance time beyond the session timeout; that 15 s is a policy value chosen before the run, not a measurement. At 30000 ms every takeover fell inside its window, so that prediction held. At 45000 ms every takeover was later than 60 s, the end of its window, so that prediction is contradicted, and with it the verdict. The run does not show that 30000 ms is the right value or that 45000 ms is wrong. It measured failover time only. It did not test the cost of a shorter timeout: a live member that stalls being removed from the group by mistake. This result describes failover time and recommends no value.

**Time beyond the session timeout.** The takeover came 1.8 to 8.6 s after the session timeout at 30000 ms, and 16.3 to 23.2 s after it at 45000 ms. The run does not show why the extra time is larger at 45000 ms.

**More takeover time, more skipped messages.** In every repeat, the skipped messages divided by the takeover time in seconds came to 2.00 to 2.14 (2.02 to 2.14 at 30000 ms, 2.00 to 2.07 at 45000 ms), so about 2 messages were skipped for each second of takeover. This is an inference from the numbers, not a measured rate.

**A shorter timeout shrinks the loss but does not remove it.** Messages were skipped at both tested values, and fewer at 30000 ms. That every session timeout leaves some loss in this case is an inference: the survivor cannot take over before the broker removes the killed member, and messages that arrive until then are skipped.

**Remedies, not tested here.** With `auto.offset.reset` `earliest`, librdkafka resets a partition with no committed offset "to the smallest offset"; with `error`, it triggers the error `ERR__AUTO_OFFSET_RESET`, which the application receives when it consumes messages (librdkafka v2.16.0 `CONFIGURATION.md` line 206). Positioning the consumer after that error is the application's choice, an inference not tested here. Neither was tested here. Inference, not tested: with `earliest` the survivor reads every message still retained on those partitions again, so processing must tolerate repeats. Storing offsets only after processing, which both consumers did here, does not prevent this case, because nothing was ever committed.

**When this happens in production.** A new group's first deployment crashes before its first commit. A group's committed offsets have expired: Microsoft lists "Consumer offsets expired beyond Event Hubs retention period" as a likely cause of an unexpected offset reset, and gives no duration. A partition was never committed to. A group that has been committing normally resumes from its last commit instead; that case is tested under C10, offset storage and crash outcomes ([scenario definition](https://github.com/collaborationwithothers/eventhubs-kafka-dotnet/blob/3e7163dc641e724437025d917032fd40419e6c4d/scenarios/C10/scenario.yaml)).

## What was tested

Each repeat ran two consumers in one group: a subject that was killed and a witness that ran the baseline consumer and received the subject's partitions. The load generator offered 4 synthetic messages per second for 150 s, 600 per repeat, in five repeats per configuration. C0 runs the baseline, with `session.timeout.ms` 30000. C1 sets `session.timeout.ms` to 45000 on the subject and changes nothing else; the witness runs the baseline in both configurations. K-57, a consumer killed before its first commit with `auto.offset.reset` at its baseline, is tested here and not under C10, because C10's two phases compare offset-store modes.

Kill point (from the expected outcome):

> The subject sends SIGKILL to itself when Consume returns its first message, before processing it, so it has committed nothing (ADR-022). heartbeat.interval.ms stays 3000. The witness runs the baseline.

Window policy (from the expected outcome):

> The lower bound is session.timeout.ms minus heartbeat.interval.ms, because the last heartbeat can precede the kill by up to 3000 ms. The upper bound adds 15000 ms for rebalance time, a policy value, not a measurement.

## Hypotheses

- H1, removal follows 30000 (C0): holds. All five takeovers, 31.8 to 38.6 s, are inside the window from 27 s up to but not including 45 s.
- H2, removal follows 45000 (C1): contradicted. All five takeovers, 61.3 to 68.2 s, are at or after 60 s, the end of the window from 42 s up to but not including 60 s.
- H3, K-57, a latest reset skips messages, published on its own line and not part of the verdict: holds in C0 and C1. Every repeat had at least one message committed but never processed (66 to 78 in C0, 125 to 141 in C1).
- Early removal, a finding on its own line: none. No takeover in either configuration came before the session timeout minus the heartbeat interval (27 s in C0, 42 s in C1).
- Takeover times, recorded only and published on their own line: as in Results by repeat.

## Commit evidence

After each repeat the verifier read the group's committed offsets from Event Hubs without joining the group (`offset_read`, outcome `success` in all ten repeats). A repeat counts only when every committed offset it read matches an offset the members' own commit records report (otherwise it is invalid), and only when, on every partition that received messages during the run, the committed offset reached the run's end line (otherwise it is incomplete). So "committed" here is observed from the broker, not only reported by the consumers. Every delivered message was processed once or never: none was processed twice.

# C03: a consumer that kept heartbeating but made no Consume call lost its partitions at the poll limit; a paused consumer that kept calling Consume kept them

In run 44562323424 on Event Hubs Premium, a consumer that processed one message inline for 90000 ms, making no `Consume` call, lost its partitions in all five repeats of C0: the other group member took over the slow message's partition 60.55 s to 62.99 s after the slow message started, after the subject's 60000 ms `max.poll.interval.ms` and not at its 30000 ms `session.timeout.ms`, and the slow message was processed a second time. In all five repeats of C1, a consumer that paused its partitions for 90000 ms but kept calling `Consume` kept them, and no message was processed twice. Both deciding hypotheses hold, so the proposed verdict is `confirmed`; no message was committed without being processed in any of the ten repeats, and the workload is synthetic.

In plain words: a consumer has two separate timers. Heartbeats are sent by a librdkafka background thread, whether or not the application calls `Consume`, and they keep the member in the group as long as one arrives within `session.timeout.ms`. `max.poll.interval.ms` is different: a `Consume` call resets it, and when the gap between calls is longer, librdkafka stops the heartbeats, leaves the group itself, and the partitions move to another member. So a consumer that blocks in its own processing code for longer than `max.poll.interval.ms` loses its partitions even though its heartbeats had kept it in the group past the session timeout, as C0 showed; a consumer that pauses its partitions and keeps calling `Consume`, as C1 did, keeps them.

Proposed verdict: `confirmed`.

## What was tested

Each repeat ran two consumers in one group: a subject that carried the slow message and a witness that ran the baseline consumer. One message, at the slow-message ordinal 100 fixed in the scenario, took the subject 90000 ms. In C0 the subject processed it inline, with no `Consume` call for 90000 ms. In C1 the subject paused its partitions for 90000 ms and kept calling `Consume`. The verifier counted 240 delivered synthetic events per repeat, in five repeats per configuration.

Scaled limit (from the expected outcome):

> The subject runs max.poll.interval.ms 60000, not the 300000 baseline, and its slow message lasts 90000 ms, not 6 minutes. session.timeout.ms stays 30000 and heartbeat.interval.ms stays 3000. The witness runs the baseline.

Microsoft's Event Hubs guidance keeps `max.poll.interval.ms` at its 300000 ms default, says it must be greater than `session.timeout.ms`, and says to increase it only if processing takes longer than 5 minutes; this run tested a 60000 ms limit with the same mechanism, not 300000 ms itself.

## Hypotheses

- H1, the poll limit loses partitions (C0): holds. In each of the five repeats the subject received a lost callback, the witness took the slow message's partition during the slow message 60.55 s to 62.99 s after it started, at or above the 45 s threshold, and a message was processed again.
- H2, a paused consumer keeps its partitions (C1, K-55): holds. In each of the five repeats the subject had no lost or revoked callback, the witness received no partition assignment after its initial one, the slow message was processed once and nothing was processed again.
- H3, no commit on partitions lost (C0, K-28), published on its own line and not part of the verdict: holds. No repeat's subject commit-callback rows record a successful commit for the lost partitions between the lost callback and the subject's next assignment.
  H3 observes only the time after the application received the lost callback. The window between the poll-limit expiry and the subject's next Consume call was not observed.
- Consumer loss, recorded only: 0 messages committed but never processed in each of the ten repeats.

The 45 s threshold is a policy value between the 30 s session timeout and the subject's 60 s poll limit. A takeover at or above it means the group did not remove the subject at the session timeout, so its heartbeats continued while it made no `Consume` call.
